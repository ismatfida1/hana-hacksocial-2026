
# Commercial audit sources gathered 2026-08-24

## OpenAI
- Business terms: https://openai.com/policies/may-2025-business-terms/
- Business data privacy: https://openai.com/business-data/
- Relevant findings: OpenAI's business terms state that customers may integrate the API into Customer Applications and make those applications available to end users. The same terms say, to the extent permitted by law, the customer retains Input ownership and owns Output, subject to responsibility for rights, accuracy, policy compliance, and data processing obligations. The business-data page states API inputs/outputs are not used for training by default, with retention controls for qualifying organizations. These terms do not transfer ownership of OpenAI's API or models to Hana.

## Google Gemini
- Gemini API Additional Terms: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms
- Google APIs Terms: https://developers.google.com/terms
- Relevant findings: Gemini terms distinguish unpaid and paid services. The terms state Google does not claim ownership of generated content, but warn that output may be similar and the developer is responsible for compliance and review. Unpaid services may use submitted content and generated responses to improve products and may involve human reviewers; the terms warn not to submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to unpaid services. Paid services have different processing commitments. Google API terms require protection of user information, an accurate privacy policy, and compliance with third-party rights; API credentials must remain confidential and must not be embedded in open-source projects.

## Manus
- Ownership FAQ: https://help.manus.im/en/articles/13125514-do-i-own-the-assets-websites-images-videos-slides-generated-via-manus
- Relevant findings: The Manus Help Center states that users own content created with Manus for personal or commercial use, subject to applicable law and third-party rights. It also warns outputs may not be unique or protectable under IP law and require review. This does not clear third-party code, package licenses, fonts, images, or services included in HANA.

## Canva
- Content License Agreement: https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/
- Licensing explained: https://www.canva.com/licensing-explained/
- Relevant findings: Canva permits many commercial uses of designs containing licensed content, but licenses are non-exclusive and standalone redistribution is restricted. Pro content is generally licensed per Canva design; Canva content should not be treated as exclusive trademark artwork. Canva Education content is non-commercial under the licensing guidance. Each actual Canva asset and account plan must be verified before inclusion in a commercial Android or web app.

## Google Play
- Account deletion requirements: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en
- Relevant findings: Google Play states that apps allowing account creation must provide an in-app path to delete the account and associated data and a web link where users can request deletion. Developers must complete Data Safety deletion questions in Play Console. HANA currently has no demonstrated complete account-deletion flow or web deletion resource.
